How to Travel China by Train: A First-Timer's Guide to High-Speed Rail

By WoW Train · November 2026 · 7 min read

China has the largest high-speed rail network in the world, and for most trips between big cities the train is faster and simpler than flying. This guide explains the trains you will meet, real journey times, how tickets work for foreign visitors and a first route by train.

The trains you need to know

  • G trains: the high-speed services, up to 350 km/h on the main lines. Most journeys in this guide are G trains.
  • D trains: also high-speed, usually a little slower, with more stops.
  • C trains: short intercity shuttles, for example between Beijing and Tianjin.
  • Z, T and K trains: conventional trains, many of them overnight with sleeper berths. Slower, but useful for long distances.

On high-speed trains there are three classes: second class (the standard seat), first class (wider seats) and business class (seats that recline almost flat).

How long the main journeys take

The shortest journey times we found for each route, on the fastest direct train (Klook data, checked 13 November 2026):

  • Beijing – Xi'an: 4h10
  • Xi'an – Chengdu: 3h05
  • Chengdu – Chongqing: 1h06
  • Shanghai – Hangzhou: 45 min
  • Shanghai – Suzhou: 21 min

  • Beijing – Tianjin: 30 min
  • Guangzhou – Shenzhen: 29 min
  • Guangzhou – Guilin: 2h04
  • Guangzhou – Hong Kong: 47 min

Times change with the day and the train, so check the live schedule for your date when you book.

Tickets and passports

  • Train tickets in mainland China are issued in the passenger's name. Foreign visitors book with their passport, and the same passport is checked at the station.
  • You can book in English on Klook with a foreign card; the ticket is linked to your passport number.
  • Check that the name and passport number on the booking match your passport exactly.

At the station

  • Big cities have several stations: Beijing South and Beijing West, Shanghai Hongqiao and Shanghai Station, Hangzhou East and Hangzhou West. Check the station on your ticket.
  • There is a security check at the entrance, like at an airport. Arrive 30 to 45 minutes before departure.
  • Boarding opens a few minutes before the train arrives and the gate closes shortly before departure, so wait in the waiting hall near your gate.
  • Stations and trains have hot water for tea or instant noodles; most high-speed trains also sell food on board.

Hong Kong by train

The Express Rail Link runs from Guangzhou South and Shenzhen to Hong Kong West Kowloon, near the centre. Border checks for mainland China and Hong Kong are both done inside West Kowloon station, so carry your passport and any visa or entry permit you need, and allow extra time.

When to book early

Trains fill up during Chinese New Year (January or February), the May Day holiday and the National Day Golden Week (1 to 7 October). In those weeks, book as soon as you know your dates.

A 10-day route by train

  • Days 1 to 3: Beijing.
  • Day 4: train to Shanghai, about 4h18 on the fastest train.
  • Days 5 and 6: Shanghai, with a half-day trip to Suzhou (21 min away).
  • Days 7 and 8: Hangzhou and West Lake, 45 min from Shanghai.
  • Days 9 and 10: Huangshan, the Yellow Mountain, 1h13 from Hangzhou.
