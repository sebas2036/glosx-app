How to Get to Zermatt by Train

By WoW Train · Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

View timetables and tickets →

A car-free village below the Matterhorn. There is no road you can drive into Zermatt, so the train is not an alternative: it is the way in. The last stretch, up the valley on the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn, is a scenic ride in its own right.

The tip worth knowing: most journeys involve one change at Visp, in the Rhône valley. From there the narrow-gauge train climbs to Zermatt in roughly 1h 10m–1h 20m. Cars have to stop at Täsch, so you do not need to bring one.

What is the route to Zermatt?

Take a train to Visp, then change to the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn to Zermatt. From Zürich the whole journey takes roughly 3h 30m–3h 45m; from Geneva roughly 3h 15m–3h 30m; from Lucerne about four hours via Bern. Trains from Visp to Zermatt run about every half hour, and the connections are timed, so a single change is usually painless.

How to make the connection?

  • 1. Board a long-distance train toward Brig and get off at Visp.
  • 2. Change to the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn on the platform provided; the connection is usually short.
  • 3. Ride up the valley to Zermatt; the station is in the village centre.

When is the best time to go?

  • July to September is the main hiking season, with the best chance of clear Matterhorn views.
  • December to April is the ski season; book trains and rooms early around holidays.
  • Mornings are usually clearer; the mountain is often in cloud by afternoon.

What to do once you are there?

  • The Gornergrat railway — a cogwheel train of roughly 30–35 minutes to a viewpoint opposite the Matterhorn.
  • Matterhorn Glacier Paradise — a cable car to one of the highest viewpoints in Europe.
  • The Riffelsee — the lake where the Matterhorn reflects in the water, reached on foot from the Gornergrat line.
  • The Glacier Express — leaves from Zermatt for St. Moritz, a famously slow and scenic ride of about eight hours.

You have seen it. Now go. The key is the change at Visp: book your train there and the rest is up the valley. Every operator, one secure checkout.
