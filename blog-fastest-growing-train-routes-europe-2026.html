The Fastest-Growing Train Routes in Europe for 2026

By WoW Train · August 2026 · 7 min read

Rail travel across Europe isn't just holding steady this year — it's surging. Bookings on some routes have jumped by 40-50% compared to last year, new sleeper trains are relaunching connections that disappeared decades ago, and a whole generation of travelers is choosing the train as part of the trip, not just a way to get there. Here's what's actually driving the numbers, and which routes are worth booking now.

Why train travel is having a moment

A few forces are pushing this at once. "Flygskam" — flight shame — has gone from a Swedish niche concept to a mainstream reason people choose rail over short-haul flights, especially for journeys under six hours. The EU's climate targets are pushing real investment into cross-border rail infrastructure. And socially, sleeper cabins and sunrise views from dining cars have become their own kind of travel content — the train isn't just transport anymore, it's part of the story people want to tell about the trip.

1. Budapest ↔ Prague

Bookings up more than 50% year over year

This is the standout number of the season. Two of Central Europe's most atmospheric capitals, connected by a comfortable EuroCity service through the Slovak countryside — no wonder it's become one of the fastest-growing routes on the continent. Both cities reward a few days each: Budapest's thermal baths and ruin bars, Prague's old town and castle district.

2. Amsterdam ↔ Brussels

Bookings up around 45% year over year

A short, frequent, high-speed hop between two of Europe's most walkable capitals — canals and museums in Amsterdam, medieval squares and comic-strip murals in Brussels. It's an easy add-on to almost any Northern Europe itinerary, and the growth here reflects how many travelers are stacking two or three cities into a single week rather than picking just one.

3. Paris ↔ Munich

A faster daytime connection between Paris and Munich launched this year, cutting a route that used to feel like an afterthought into a genuine one-day option — French elegance to Bavarian beer halls and Alpine views, no flight required.
