How rail travel works in Italy

Trenitalia runs the Frecciarossa, Frecciargento and Frecciabianca fast trains, the Intercity trains and the regional trains. Italo is a private operator that competes on the main high-speed line between Turin, Milan, Bologna, Florence, Rome and Naples.

Fast trains have assigned seats, so you buy a specific departure. Regional trains (Regionale) have no seat reservation, and a regional paper ticket must be validated at the machine before boarding.

Most visitors use the north–south spine: Milan, Bologna, Florence, Rome and Naples, linked by frequent high-speed trains.

Train types explained

  • Frecciarossa: Trenitalia high-speed trains on the main lines, the fastest option between big cities.
  • Italo: Private high-speed trains on the same corridor, with their own fares and stations in some cities.
  • Frecciargento / Frecciabianca: Fast trains that mix high-speed and conventional lines.
  • Intercity / Regionale: Slower trains for shorter trips and smaller towns, with the lowest fares.

Main train stations

The main stations are Roma Termini, Milano Centrale, Firenze Santa Maria Novella, Venezia Santa Lucia and Napoli Centrale. Some cities have a second station, so check the name on your ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to reserve Italian high-speed trains?

Yes. Frecciarossa and Italo tickets are for a specific train and seat. Regional trains do not need a reservation.

Do I have to validate my train ticket in Italy?

Paper regional tickets must be validated at the station machine before you board. Tickets bought for a specific fast train, and e-tickets, do not need it.

Which Italian cities are best connected by train?

Milan, Bologna, Florence, Rome and Naples sit on the main high-speed line and have the most frequent departures.
