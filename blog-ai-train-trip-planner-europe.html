How to Plan a European Train Trip with AI (2026 Guide)

By WoW Train · July 2026 · 6 min read

Planning a multi-city train trip across Europe the old way means a dozen browser tabs, three currencies, two languages you don't speak, and an evening lost figuring out which city needs a connection and which one doesn't. An AI trip planner does all of that in the time it takes to type one sentence. Here's how it actually works, and how to get a genuinely useful itinerary out of it.

Why planning a European rail trip by hand is so slow

It isn't that any single step is hard. It's that a train trip through 3-4 countries touches a dozen small decisions at once: which operator runs each leg, whether a connection needs 20 minutes or 90, which cities are worth a hotel night versus a same-day change, and whether a "direct" train is actually direct or has a change hidden in the fine print. Doing that research manually for even a 5-stop trip easily eats two or three hours, spread across Trainline, Rail Europe, individual operator sites and a stack of blog posts that are a year out of date.

What an AI planner actually does differently

Instead of researching each leg separately, you describe the whole trip in one go — cities, rough dates, pace — and the AI builds the full route at once: which trains connect each city, realistic journey times, and where it makes sense to stop overnight instead of racing through. A good planner also suggests a hotel near each stop and links directly to book both the train and the room, so the itinerary you get is something you can actually act on, not just a list of city names.

How to write a prompt that gets a useful itinerary

The output is only as good as the input. Three things make a real difference:

  • Name your cities in order, or let the AI sequence them. "Paris, then Amsterdam, then Berlin" works. So does "a week in France and the Netherlands" if you're happy to have the route suggested for you.
  • Give a rough trip length. "10 days" changes the plan completely from "3 days" — the AI needs to know how much ground it can cover before it decides how many stops to include.
  • Say what kind of trip it is. A honeymoon, a backpacking trip and a work-plus-weekend trip should not get the same pace. Mention it and the itinerary adjusts — fewer, longer stops for a relaxed trip; more cities for a fast-paced one.

What to check before you book anything

Treat the AI itinerary as a very strong first draft, not a final confirmation. Before booking, always verify the live departure time and price directly with the operator — schedules shift, and an AI planner works from general route knowledge, not a live timetable feed. The itinerary's job is to save you the hours of research that get you to "here's the plan," not to replace the final booking step.
