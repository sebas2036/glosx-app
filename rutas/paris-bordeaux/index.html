Worth knowing

The journey time dropped from well over 3 hours to about 2 when a new high-speed line (LGV Sud Europe Atlantique) opened in 2017 — one of the last major LGV lines built in France, and financed through a public-private partnership rather than by the state alone, an unusual model for a project of this size.

Stations: where you leave and where you arrive

  • Paris Montparnasse is the departure station for the Atlantic high-speed line, with Metro lines 4, 6, 12 and 13.
  • Bordeaux Saint-Jean is about 3 km south of the centre. The tram connects it with the Place de la Bourse and the old town in roughly 15–20 minutes.

Paris to Bordeaux train times and frequency

Since the high-speed line to Bordeaux opened in 2017, the fastest TGV trains take a little over two hours. Departures are frequent through the day, from early morning to the evening, so a day trip in either direction is realistic, although a night in Bordeaux is much more relaxed.

Best time to travel

Fridays, Sundays and summer holidays are the busiest and priciest. Mid-morning midweek trains are usually cheapest. Bordeaux is also popular in the wine harvest weeks, so book accommodation early.

What to do when you arrive in Bordeaux

Bordeaux is a handsome, walkable city. Highlights:

  • Place de la Bourse and the Miroir d'eau — the 18th-century square and the reflecting pool on the quays.
  • La Cité du Vin — a museum dedicated to the world of wine, on the river.
  • The Chartrons district — antiques, cafés and wine merchants.
  • Saint-Émilion — a medieval wine village, about 35 minutes by train from Bordeaux.

Other ways to get from Paris to Bordeaux

  • Coach — services take roughly seven hours, much slower than the train.
  • Car — about five and a half hours plus tolls.
  • Flying — about an hour in the air, but with transfers and security the train is competitive.
