How rail travel works in Spain

Renfe is the national operator and runs the AVE high-speed trains, the cheaper Avlo, and the Alvia, Euromed, Intercity and Media Distancia services. Ouigo and Iryo compete on the busiest high-speed lines, including Madrid–Barcelona, Madrid–Valencia and Madrid–Andalusia.

High-speed and long-distance tickets are for a specific train and seat, so you book a departure, not just a route. Fares usually rise as the date gets closer.

Madrid and Barcelona are the main hubs. Most high-speed lines leave from Madrid; the Barcelona–Madrid, Barcelona–Valencia and Madrid–Valencia lines are among the busiest.

Train types explained

  • AVE: Renfe high-speed trains, the fastest option between the big cities.
  • Avlo, Ouigo, Iryo: Lower-fare high-speed services; Ouigo and Iryo are private operators that compete with Renfe.
  • Alvia / Euromed: Fast long-distance trains that use both high-speed and conventional lines.
  • Media Distancia / Cercanías: Regional and commuter trains for shorter trips.

Main train stations

In Madrid, high-speed trains leave from Atocha or Chamartín; in Barcelona from Sants; in Valencia the high-speed station is Joaquín Sorolla; in Seville Santa Justa. Check the departure station on your ticket, because big cities have more than one.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book Spanish high-speed trains in advance?

Yes, you need a ticket for a specific train. Booking early usually gives lower fares, especially on routes shared by Renfe, Ouigo and Iryo.

Is there a security check at Spanish train stations?

High-speed and long-distance trains from the main stations have a baggage check, so arrive a few minutes earlier than for a regional train.
