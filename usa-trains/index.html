Trains in the United States

Amtrak is the national passenger railroad. Its busiest line is the Northeast Corridor between Boston, New York, Philadelphia and Washington, served by Acela and Northeast Regional trains.

Outside the Northeast, frequent trains run on state-supported corridors such as the Pacific Surfliner (San Diego–Los Angeles), the Capitol Corridor (Sacramento–Bay Area), Gold Runner (Bay Area/Sacramento–Bakersfield) and the lines from Chicago to Milwaukee, St. Louis and Detroit. In Florida, the private operator Brightline links Miami, Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach and Orlando.

Long-distance trains such as the California Zephyr, the Lake Shore Limited or the City of New Orleans usually run once a day and carry sleeping cars.

How to book

Tickets are sold for a specific train, and fares usually rise as the date gets closer. You can book on the operator's website or compare times and fares on Omio from each route guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book Amtrak trains in advance?

Most Amtrak trains need a ticket for a specific train, and fares usually rise as the date gets closer, so booking ahead tends to cost less.

What is the fastest train in the United States?

Acela, on the Northeast Corridor between Boston, New York, Philadelphia and Washington.

Is there a train between Miami and Orlando?

Yes. Brightline runs direct trains between Miami and Orlando, with stops in Fort Lauderdale and West Palm Beach.

Which station do trains use in New York?

Amtrak trains in New York use Penn Station, including Moynihan Train Hall.
