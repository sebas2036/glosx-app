How rail travel works in Germany

Deutsche Bahn (DB) runs most long-distance trains and the national ticket system. Regional trains are run by DB Regio and other regional operators, and FlixTrain operates a few long-distance lines in competition.

Most city-to-city journeys use the ICE high-speed network (up to 300 km/h on the fastest lines) or IC/EC intercity trains. Regional trains (RE, RB) and city railways (S-Bahn) are slower and stop more often.

DB sells Sparpreis fares (cheaper, tied to a specific train) and Flexpreis fares (valid on any train that day). Regional trains are also covered by the monthly Deutschland-Ticket.

Train types explained

  • ICE: High-speed long-distance trains between the big cities.
  • IC / EC: Intercity and EuroCity trains on slower main lines and to neighbouring countries.
  • RE / RB: Regional express and regional trains: slower, many stops, the cheapest way for short trips.
  • S-Bahn: Suburban railways inside the big city regions (Berlin, Munich, Frankfurt, Hamburg, Cologne).

Main train stations

The main stations are Berlin Hbf, München Hbf, Frankfurt (Main) Hbf, Köln Hbf and Hamburg Hbf (Hbf stands for Hauptbahnhof, the central station). Frankfurt Airport has its own long-distance station.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to reserve a seat on German trains?

Not for travel, but a seat reservation is optional on ICE and IC trains and worth adding at busy times. A Sparpreis ticket is valid only on the train you booked.

What is the fastest way between German cities?

The ICE. On the main lines it is faster than driving between large cities such as Frankfurt, Cologne and Berlin.

Where can I buy German train tickets?

On the Deutsche Bahn site and app, at station machines and counters, or through booking platforms such as Omio, which also sell tickets for trains in other countries.
